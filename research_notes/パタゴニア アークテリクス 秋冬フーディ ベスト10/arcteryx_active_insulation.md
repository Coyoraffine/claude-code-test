## Which Arc'teryx active-insulation pieces are in the 2026 lineup and how have they changed?

### Takeaway
The current (FW2025–FW2026) lineup has two families, each with three weights. Atom is the warmer, more wind-resistant everyday family: Atom SL, Atom, Atom SV. Proton is the air-permeable active family: Proton SL, Proton, Proton Heavyweight. Nuclei Hoody is a separate windproof, packable alpine piece. Model names changed twice. In 2023 "LT" was dropped (Atom LT → Atom, Proton LT → Proton) and "AR/FL" became "Heavyweight/Lightweight". For 2025, "Lightweight" went back to "SL" and "Atom Heavyweight" became "Atom SV".

### Inferences
- For a "Patagonia Capilene Cool Daily Hoody but thicker" buyer, the realistic current candidates are Proton Hoody, Proton SL Hoody, Atom SL Hoody and Atom Hoody. Atom SV and Proton Heavyweight are cold-mountain pieces. The Nuclei Hoody is a windproof, packable alpine belay piece and the least breathable of the group.
- The Proton SL is not the lightest piece despite its name. At 370 g it is close to the regular Proton and the Atom Hoody. Its "SL" refers to a slimmer, more trim spec, not to minimal weight. The Atom SL (≈280 g) is the lightest model.
- The model names in Japanese articles from 2022–2024 (アトム LT, アトム AR, プロトン LT, プロトン AR, ライトウェイト, ヘビーウェイト) do not match the current names. Any comparison article should map old names to new ones.

## Which are genuinely breathable (air-permeable) vs. warm but potentially clammy?

### Takeaway
The Proton family (Fortius Air face fabric) is the air-permeable "breathe-first" line. Reviewers consistently say it vents heat and moisture well while moving, at the cost of wind-through and less warmth at rest. Atom (Tyono 20/30 face) is warmer and more wind-resistant at rest but runs hot and can get clammy at high output. The revised Atom SL now also uses Fortius Air 20, which makes it the most breathable Atom.

### Cited Findings
- Switchback Travel says breathability is one of the compromises of the Atom's efficient warmth, and that the Atom runs fairly warm during high-output sports such as ski touring compared with active-insulation jackets like the Proton. — [Switchback Travel Atom Hoody review](https://Switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-atom-hoody)
- A Mountain Project user who owns both said the Atom "does not wick or breathe very well", and recommended a Nano-Air if breathability matters more. — [Mountain Project forum](https://www.mountainproject.com/forum/message/200512022)
- Switchback's synthetic roundup says the Proton Hoody "isn't great at blocking wind" but "excels during high-output pursuits like ski touring, cross-country skiing, cold-weather trail runs, and brisk winter hikes". It "can feel too warm during mild-weather outings or when you're pushing your heart rate into the red". — [Switchback best synthetic jackets](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/best-synthetic-insulated-jackets)
- In Switchback's Proton LT (predecessor) review, sweat soaked the inside after about 10 minutes of maximum output. Its breathability was rated close to the "less breathable" Atom LT, and its wind protection improved over the prior generation. — [Switchback Proton LT review](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody)
- BetterTrail calls the Proton Hoody "exceptionally breathable during movement" but "vulnerable to wind, limiting its effectiveness as a static insulator". — [BetterTrail Proton Hoody review](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/arcteryx-proton-hoody-review)
- PowderGuide (Proton LT) confirmed the Proton's higher breathability and slightly higher wind permeability than the Atom, so it is slightly less warm than the Atom in wind. — [PowderGuide Proton LT](https://powderguide.com/en/magazine/materialtests/gear-reviews-arc-teryx-proton-lt-hoody)
- Off-Piste describes the Proton Hoody as "a light, airy insulated jacket" that stays breathable enough to prevent overheating and sweating. — [Off-Piste Mag](https://offpistemag.com/arcteryx-proton-hoody/)
- Fortius Air 20 is described as an air-permeable 20-denier plain-weave nylon. One review says the Proton's Fortius Air shell is more breathable but less durable and less wind-resistant than the Atom's Tyono shell. — [OutdoorCrunch Proton vs Atom](https://outdoorcrunch.com/arcteryx-proton-vs-atom/); [Extrabux Atom vs Proton vs Nuclei](https://www.extrabux.com/en/guide/8127264)
- Zappos owners are mixed on the Proton's wind resistance. One calls it "very well wind resistant" with acceptable breathability, while another says a cold breeze can sneak through. — [Zappos Proton Hoody reviews](https://www.zappos.com/product/review/9961445)
- Japanese reviewers: the Proton has high breathability and sweat rarely builds up when active. The Atom heats up during activity and moves sweat less well than the Proton. — [note「はるか」Proton review](https://note.com/harufure3/n/nceb788b53b05). Another owner says the Atom's side panels vent well and they rarely felt clammy. — [harufure.jp Atom LT review](https://harufure.jp/atom-lt-hoody/)
- The Atom SL is a cool-weather, high-output layer. One reviewer likened it to "a thin fleece paired with a wind shirt" and said it is "not warm for inactive use below the low 50s°F" (~10–11°C). — [search extract of BetterTrail / Kootenay Mountain Culture Atom SL reviews](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/arcteryx-atom-sl-hoody-synthetic-insulated-jacket-review); [Kootenay Mountain Culture](https://kootenaymountainculture.com/honest-review-arcteryx-atom-sl-hoody/)
- The Proton SL places Octa knit insulation right against the base layer under a thin shell, and has "no rain protection". — [Brooks Review (Mar 2026)](https://brooksreview.net/2026/03/arcteryx-proton-sl-hoody/)
- The Nuclei Hoody uses an Arato 10r "windproof" ripstop shell. — [arcteryx.com Nuclei](https://arcteryx.com/us/en/shop/mens/nuclei-hoody?bvstate=pg:3/ct:r)

### Inferences
- Breathability ranking, from most to least air-permeable (inferred from materials and review consensus, not lab CFM data):
  1. Proton SL (Octa knit, Fortius Air 20)
  2. Proton Hoody (Coreloft Compact 80, Fortius Air 20)
  3. Atom SL (Fortius Air 20, thin, uninsulated sleeves)
  4. Proton Heavyweight (Fortius Air 50, 100 g/m²)
  5. Atom Hoody (Tyono 20)
  6. Atom SV (Tyono 30, 120 g/m²)
  7. Nuclei Hoody (windproof Arato)
- Octa is a knit, open-structured insulation (similar in concept to Polartec Alpha), which is the most likely reason Proton SL is regarded as the best "moving" piece. This is an inference from the "knitted insulation" description in [OutdoorCrunch](https://outdoorcrunch.com/synthetic-insulated-jackets/).
- Down (e.g., Cerium) is less breathable for active use: down jackets use tightly woven, down-proof, low-air-permeability shells, and down loses loft when damp with sweat. This is background knowledge and was not sourced in this session. Switchback notes the Proton LT falls well short of the Cerium LT in warmth-for-weight. That is the trade-off: down is warmer per gram at rest but not suited to high-output use. — [Switchback Proton LT review](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody)

### Gaps
- No published air-permeability (CFM) numbers for Tyono 20, Fortius Air 20, Fortius Air 50 or Octa were found, so the ranking is qualitative.
- No independent lab test of clamminess for the 2025-revised Atom SL (Fortius Air version) was found.

## Suitability as a single outer layer for 0–12°C Japanese town/commuting vs. as an active layer

### Takeaway
For 0–12°C town and commuting use as the only outer layer, the Atom Hoody is the consensus pick in Japanese reviews: it is warmer at rest, blocks light wind, and its side panels help with heat on trains. The Proton family is better as an active layer (hiking, running, cycling) or under a shell. Used alone in windy city cold, it lets wind through and feels cooler when standing still.

### Cited Findings
- Several Japanese reviews agree: the Atom suits town wear and the Proton suits mid-layer use. The Atom is warmer, while the Proton lets wind through and is less suited to use as an outer layer. — [harufure.jp Atom review](https://harufure.jp/atom-lt-hoody/); [note「はるか」Proton review](https://note.com/harufure3/n/nceb788b53b05); [toshi314-hakui.blog Atom vs Proton](https://toshi314-hakui.blog/%E3%80%90%E3%82%A2%E3%83%BC%E3%82%AF%E3%83%86%E3%83%AA%E3%82%AF%E3%82%B9%E3%80%91%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%97%E3%81%AE%E5%8C%96%E7%B9%8A%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B5%E3%83%AC%E3%83%BC%E3%82%B7%E3%83%A7%E3%83%B3/); [tongari-tent.blue Atom summary](https://tongari-tent.blue/atom_matome/)

### Inferences
- These temperature ranges are inferred from the reviews above, not tested by sources:
  - **Commuting at 5–12°C** (Tokyo/Osaka daytime Nov–Feb): Atom Hoody over a T-shirt or light base is likely enough. Overheating on crowded trains is manageable via the full zip.
  - **Commuting at 0–5°C, or windy days:** the Atom Hoody is borderline when standing still. Atom SV or a layer underneath is safer.
  - The Proton Hoody alone at 0–5°C in wind will likely feel cold when stationary.
- For a user whose main complaint is sweatiness:
  - Proton Hoody or Proton SL for walking-heavy days, hiking, cycling and running.
  - Atom SL for mild days (8–15°C) or running, given its light weight and Fortius Air shell.
  - Atom Hoody for static, cold, windy commuting.
  - A Proton plus a light windshell gives the best breathe-and-block combination. This is a layering inference, not sourced.
- The Proton SL feels closest to the Capilene Cool Daily (insulation next to the skin, knit structure, stretchy face fabric). This is a qualitative inference from the Brooks Review description.

## What do reviewers and users say about clamminess and warmth-to-weight?

### Takeaway
Reviewers praise the Atom's warmth-to-weight for a synthetic but say it gets clammy at high output. The Proton is praised for staying dry while moving but criticized for wind-through and lower static warmth. None of these pieces matches down for warmth-to-weight. Common complaints are fit changes across versions, Fortius Air being less durable than Tyono, and inconsistent weight specs.

### Cited Findings
- **Atom, warmth-to-weight:** GearJunkie praises "surprising warmth" at 13 oz. Switchback says breathability is the trade-off for the Atom's efficient warmth. — [GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/apparel/arcteryx-atom-hoody-review); [Switchback Atom](https://Switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-atom-hoody)
- **Atom, clamminess:** "does not wick or breathe very well" (Mountain Project user). A Japanese reviewer says it heats up when active and sheds sweat less well than the Proton. — [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/forum/message/200512022); [note「はるか」](https://note.com/harufure3/n/nceb788b53b05)
- **Atom, counterpoint:** a Japanese owner rarely felt clammy thanks to the side-panel ventilation. — [harufure.jp](https://harufure.jp/atom-lt-hoody/)
- **Proton, clamminess:** "exceptionally breathable during movement" (BetterTrail). "light, airy … breathable enough to prevent overheating and sweating" (Off-Piste). Switchback, however, found the Proton LT's interior soaked after about 10 minutes at maximum output. — [BetterTrail](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/arcteryx-proton-hoody-review); [Off-Piste](https://offpistemag.com/arcteryx-proton-hoody/); [Switchback Proton LT](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody)
- **Proton, warmth-to-weight:** falls well short of a down jacket like the 10.8 oz Cerium LT. — [Switchback Proton LT](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody)
- **Proton durability:** Arc'teryx claims the Fortius face fabric is over 60 times more durable in abrasion tests than the industry standard (Proton LT era). Other reviews call Fortius Air less durable than Tyono. — [backpackersmentality (Arc'teryx claim)](https://backpackersmentality.com/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody-review/); [Extrabux](https://www.extrabux.com/en/guide/8127264)
- **Atom SL, 2025 revision:** the lined StormHood can no longer be stowed (a feature loss noted on arcteryx.jp). — [arcteryx.jp Atom SL](https://arcteryx.jp/products/atom-sl-hoody-m)
- **Proton family and active use:** the Proton Hoody "can feel too warm during mild-weather outings or when you're pushing your heart rate into the red". — [Switchback roundup](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/best-synthetic-insulated-jackets)
- **Sizing:** a Japanese store blog with staff trying every size exists for the Atom range ([arknets 全サイズ着用](https://www.arknets.co.jp/storestaff/blogdetail.aspx?article_id=771554)), and Japanese sizing guides ([SUNDAY MOUNTAIN](https://www.sundaymountain.jp/ready-outdoor/arcteryx-atom/); [jumpei-blog](https://jumpei-blog.com/arcteryx-atomheavyweighthoody-size-review/)) are available. The 2023 rename came with an updated fit. — [Treeline / BetterTrail extract: "updated the fit, DWR chemistry and construction details"](https://www.treelinereview.com/gearreviews/best-synthetic-insulated-jackets)

### Inferences
- For a user who found the Capilene Cool Daily comfortable because it does not trap sweat, the Proton line best matches that priority. The Atom Hoody is the "warm in town" choice and accepts some clamminess on fast walks or stairs.
- On warmth-to-weight, the Atom SL (~280 g) and Atom Hoody (~355 g) are the most efficient. The Proton trades about 20–60 g extra (375–415 g reported) for breathability.

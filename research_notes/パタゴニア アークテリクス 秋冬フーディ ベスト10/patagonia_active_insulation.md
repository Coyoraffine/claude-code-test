## Q1. Which Patagonia active-insulation pieces are in the 2026 lineup, and how have they changed?

### Takeaway
For FW2026 Patagonia reorganised the Nano-Air family into three insulation levels: 20 g (Nano-Air Ultralight Full-Zip Hoody, launched 2025), 40 g (Nano-Air Light Hoody, heavily redesigned for FW2026), and 60 g (now a lifestyle piece, the new Nano-Air Shirt Jacket). The classic 60 g Nano-Air Hoody (2014 original concept) has been dropped. The "puffy" synthetics (Nano Puff, Micro Puff, DAS Light) continue as separate, warmer-but-less-breathable lines.

### Cited Findings

**Overall restructuring (FW2026)**
- FW2026 Nano-Air comes in three insulation weights: 20 g, 40 g and 60 g (g/m² of FullRange fill). The 20 g "Nano-Air Ultralight" appeared in 2025, and this season the 40 g "Nano-Air Light" was relaunched with major improvements. — [BE-PAL「パタゴニア『ナノエア』が進化！」](https://www.bepal.net/archives/739219); [Yamakei Online media-event report](https://www.yamakei-online.com/journal/detail.php?id=8636); [Trail Runner.JP「3つの保温レベルで展開」](https://trailrunner.jp/68669)
- The 40 g and 60 g FullRange insulations got vent openings ("通気口") to release excess heat during activity, while reportedly keeping warmth in still air or under a shell. The lining is now more air-permeable than the face fabric so body heat and vapour move outward. — [BE-PAL](https://www.bepal.net/archives/739219); [Yamakei Online「通気孔をもつ『新中綿』で蒸れずに着続けられる最適解｜ナノエア・ライト・フーディ」](https://www.yamakei-online.com/yama-ya/detail.php?id=5160)

## Q2. Which are genuinely breathable (air-permeable) vs. warm-but-clammy?

### Takeaway
Breathability ranking is consistent across reviewers: Nano-Air (Ultralight > Light > old 60 g) is far more air-permeable than either Puff. The Micro Puff (Pertex Quantum) breathes slightly better than the Nano Puff (denser ripstop with DWR), and the Nano Puff is the most likely to go clammy during exertion. The trade-off is the reverse for wind and rain: Nano-Air lets wind through and is not a rain layer.

### Cited Findings
- Comparisons rank Nano-Air most breathable, then Micro Puff, then Nano Puff. Nano-Air is "a far more breathable option than the Micro Puff for a layer you leave on while active" and "dumps excess heat quickly". — [Switchback Travel Micro Puff review](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/patagonia-micro-puff-hoody); [Outdoor Crunch Nano-Air vs Nano Puff](https://outdoorcrunch.com/nano-air-vs-nano-puff/); [Engearment Micro Puff vs Nano Puff vs Nano Air](https://engearment.com/backcountry/patagonia-micro-puff-vs-nano-puff-vs-nano-air/)
- One of these comparison articles cites ~40 CFM breathability for the (older, 60 g) Nano-Air. I could not tell which article from the search summary. — [Outdoor Crunch](https://outdoorcrunch.com/nano-air-vs-nano-puff/) / [Engearment](https://engearment.com/backcountry/patagonia-micro-puff-vs-nano-puff-vs-nano-air/)
- The Nano-Air Ultralight is rated 63.2 CFM by Patagonia. — [Vertical Life](https://verticallifemag.com/articles/patagonia-nano-air-ultralight-hoody/)
- The Micro Puff is "a bit more breathable than the Nano Puff" thanks to its lighter face fabric, and "breathes a touch better during hard efforts, while the Nano Puff can feel clammy sooner". The Nano Puff "doesn't breathe that much" but is better at shedding water and resisting abrasion and wind. — [Outdoor Crunch Nano Puff vs Micro Puff](https://outdoorcrunch.com/nano-puff-vs-micro-puff/); [Gearlow](https://www.gearlow.com/brands/patagonia-nano-puff-vs-micro-puff/); [Treeline Review Micro Puff](https://www.treelinereview.com/gearreviews/patagonia-micro-puff-jacket-review)
- Nano-Air weather limits: "we don't recommend the Nano-Air as an outer layer even in light rain", and it is "not very wind resistant". — [Engearment](https://engearment.com/backcountry/patagonia-micro-puff-vs-nano-puff-vs-nano-air/); [Outdoor Crunch](https://outdoorcrunch.com/nano-air-vs-nano-puff/)
- FW2026 Nano-Air Light Hoody: "wind resistance is better than expected but still takes a back seat to breathability". Pair it with a breathable wind or rain shell in any sort of extreme weather. Its design goal is to shed heat rather than hold it. — [BetterTrail Nano-Air Light Hoody review (Oct 26, 2026)](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-synthetic-insulated-jacket-review)
- Gripped (fall 2026) lists mobility, ventilation and a sleek fit as the Nano-Air Light Hoody's key strengths. — [Gripped](https://gripped.com/gear/the-patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-is-perfect-for-fall/)
- Patagonia Japan says the vented FW2026 FullRange plus a more permeable lining keeps you from getting stuffy (蒸れずに) without taking the layer on and off. — [Yamakei Online](https://www.yamakei-online.com/yama-ya/detail.php?id=5160); [BE-PAL](https://www.bepal.net/archives/739219)
- Nano-Air Light Hybrid (older, R1 Air knit back/sides): "in anything less breathable than this jacket I'd have got quite sweaty". But the super-breathable back plus wind-resistant front can make it "balloon in steady wind from behind" without a pack. — [SectionHiker](https://sectionhiker.com/patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-review/); [The Great Outdoors](https://www.thegreatoutdoorsmag.com/review/patagonia-nano-air-light-hybrid-hoody/); [FeedTheHabit](https://www.feedthehabit.com/patagonia-nano-air-light-hybrid-hoody-review/). Men's Journal named it a top pick in its Outdoor Awards for breathable active insulation and stretch. — [Men's Journal](https://www.mensjournal.com/gear/patagonia-nano-air-hybrid-hoody-jacket-gear-review)

### Inferences
- For a user whose reference point is the Capilene Cool Daily Hoody and who hates clamminess, the Nano-Air Ultralight (20 g, 63.2 CFM) and the FW2026 Nano-Air Light (40 g, vented) are the only Patagonia insulated pieces designed for that. Nano Puff, Micro Puff and DAS Light are "static warmth" pieces.
- Down jackets (e.g. the Down Sweater and the new Durable Down Hoody mentioned in FW2026 previews) need tightly woven, down-proof shells to hold feathers in, so they are generally less air-permeable than Nano-Air. This is a general-principle inference, not tested in the sources above.

### Gaps
- No independent lab CFM measurements for any FW2026 Patagonia model. The 63.2 CFM is a Patagonia figure via one reviewer.
- No published CFM for the Nano Puff, Micro Puff or DAS Light shells.
- No direct reviewer test of the FW2026 Nano-Air Light Hoody for "clamminess" (BetterTrail and Gripped discuss ventilation qualitatively).

## Q4. Suitability as a single outer layer for 0–12 °C Japanese winter town/commuting vs. hiking/running/cycling

### Takeaway
Nano-Air pieces are designed as layers worn while moving under or without a shell. As a lone outer layer in 0–12 °C town use they work in calm, dry weather, but wind passes through them and they aren't rain layers. For static urban wear in 0–5 °C wind, a Nano Puff or Micro Puff (more wind-resistant shells) will feel warmer. For brisk walking, cycling, running or hiking, Nano-Air Ultralight or Light is the right choice to avoid clamminess.

### Cited Findings
- Patagonia Japan positions 60 g (Shirt Jacket) as "everyday wear" and 40 g as the mountain all-rounder. 20 g is for high sweat output. — [BE-PAL](https://www.bepal.net/archives/739219); [YouTube short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/zWhr8RFfakU)
- Nano-Air Light Hoody: pair it with a breathable wind or rain shell in any sort of extreme weather. — [BetterTrail](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-synthetic-insulated-jacket-review)
- Nano-Air "not very wind resistant" and not recommended as an outer layer even in light rain. — [Engearment](https://engearment.com/backcountry/patagonia-micro-puff-vs-nano-puff-vs-nano-air/); [Outdoor Crunch](https://outdoorcrunch.com/nano-air-vs-nano-puff/)
- The Nano-Air Ultralight was found more wind-resistant than an R1 Pullover fleece. — [Vertical Life](https://verticallifemag.com/articles/patagonia-nano-air-ultralight-hoody/)
- Real-world commuting: a Tom's Guide writer uses the Nano Puff as his main outer jacket for winter bike commuting in Seattle at around 45 °F (~7 °C). — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/wellness/fitness/the-patagonia-nano-puff-is-so-cozy-i-want-to-sleep-in-it-and-its-now-50-percent-off-for-cyber-monday). A user says the Nano Puff "gets uncomfortably warm for me in the mid to upper 40s" °F (~7–9 °C). — [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/19342623-patagonia-men-s-nano-puff-jacket-sizzle-red-117-27) (search summary)
- A user wears the Nano-Air as a ski second layer and calls it "fantastic in adding warmth while being breathable". — [Slickdeals](https://slickdeals.net/f/17312800-patagonia-men-s-nano-air-light-hybrid-jacket-limited-sizes-from-124-free-shipping)
- Patagonia's own description: the stretchy insulation "dumps excess heat when you're working hard and keeps you warm when you're not". — [Patagonia Nano-Air collection](https://www.patagonia.com/shop/favorites/nano-air/s) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Japanese town commuting usually means brisk walking plus stations and trains (often over-heated). That stop-and-go heat pattern favours Nano-Air. The best single-piece compromise for 5–12 °C dry days is the FW2026 Nano-Air Light Hoody (40 g, new Regular fit for layering, two chest pockets plus hood). For 0–5 °C windy days in town it likely needs a windshell over it, or a Nano Puff or Micro Puff instead.
- The Nano-Air Ultralight (20 g) is closest to the Capilene Cool Daily experience in feel but likely too cool for 0–5 °C as an outer layer. It is better for running, cycling and hiking, or as a mid-layer. Its lack of hand pockets and its form-fitting cut make it less town-friendly.
- The Nano-Air Shirt Jacket (60 g) is the warmest Nano-Air and is billed for daily wear, but it has no hood, so it falls outside a "hoodie" list.
- The Nano Puff Hoody is the most town-practical (DWR ripstop, wind-resistant, durable, cheapest), but it is the most likely to get clammy when walking fast or on heated trains.
- Typical Tokyo and Osaka Dec–Feb daytime highs of roughly 8–12 °C, with mornings near 0–5 °C, are general climate background, not sourced in this session.

## Q5. What reviewers and users say about clamminess and warmth-to-weight

### Takeaway
Reviewers agree that Nano-Air trades warmth-to-weight for breathability. It feels "so-so" for static warmth but stays dry while moving. Puffs give better warmth per gram but go clammy sooner under exertion, the Nano Puff most of all. FW2026 Japanese press is positive about the vented redesign, but independent long-term tests of the new Light Hoody are still scarce (as of Nov 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Nano-Air Light: warmth-to-weight "only so-so". It is meant to shed heat rather than hold it. — [BetterTrail](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-synthetic-insulated-jacket-review)

## 1. Independent head-to-head rankings and scores (Patagonia and Arc'teryx models only)

### Takeaway
Across 2025–2026 fleece roundups, the **Patagonia R1 Air Full-Zip Hoody** is the most common "Best Overall / best performance fleece". The **Arc'teryx Kyanite Hoody** is the main rival: warmer and better in wind, but less breathable. Switchback rates Kyanite Best Overall and R1 Air the favorite performance fleece. The **Arc'teryx Delta** is rated the more breathable, lighter and less warm option. For synthetic active insulation:
- **Atom** wins on durability and weather.
- **Nano-Air** and **Proton (LT/Lightweight)** win on breathability. Nano-Air is usually judged slightly more breathable, Proton more durable and wind-resistant.
- **Nano Puff** is repeatedly called the clammiest ("a sauna").

### Inferences
- For "thicker than a Capilene Cool Daily but not clammy", the independent consensus points first to **grid or air-knit fleece (R1 Air, Delta, Kyanite Lightweight)** and **air-permeable active insulation (Nano-Air / Nano-Air Light, Proton / Proton Lightweight)**. It does not point to Nano Puff or Micro Puff-style puffies.
- Within fleece, the observed warmth ladder is roughly **Delta < R1 Air < Kyanite (Lightweight) < Kyanite Hoody / R1 Thermal / R2 TechFace**. The breathability ladder runs in the opposite direction. The R1 Thermal and R2 TechFace positions are inferred from Japanese media descriptions, not tested scores.
- Most English scores were set on versions before 2025–2026. The Atom fit changed in 2023, the Nano-Air was updated for 2024, and the Proton names changed from LT/FL/AR to Lightweight/Heavyweight, so treat older scores as directional only.

## 3. Science and technical basis for "warm but not sweaty"

### Takeaway
"Active" garments work by letting air and vapour move through the whole garment. Breathability of the shell is usually measured as **air permeability in CFM** (ASTM D737 / Frazier test).
- Patagonia publishes **35–40 CFM for the Nano-Air** (FullRange 60 g).
- Fleece is roughly **200 CFM** by one reviewer's comparison.
- Windproof shell fabrics are about **2 CFM** in a forum mention.
- Polartec Alpha has no official CFM.
The low CFM of tight downproof or windproof shells (Nano Puff, Micro Puff, down) is why they feel clammy under exertion.

### Cited Findings
- **Nano-Air**: retailers quote Patagonia's 60 g FullRange as delivering "outstanding warmth, mechanical stretch and air permeability (35 CFM)" for the hoody. Reviews cite **40 CFM** for the jacket and vest, measured by **ASTM D737** / the **Frazier Air Permeability Test**, and compare that with fleece at **about 200 CFM**. One review mentions an unverified independent test above **170 CFM**. — [Landfall Navigation listing](https://www.landfallnavigation.com/patagonia-nano-air-hoody-mens-84367.html); [Gear Patrol Nano-Air](https://www.gearpatrol.com/outdoors/a108182/review-patagonia-nano-air-jacket/); [Gearist Nano-Air vest](https://www.gearist.com/patagonia-nano-air-vest-review/); [Engearment](https://engearment.com/backcountry/patagonia-nano-air-best-mid-layer-jacket-ever/)
- **Backpacking Light**: the original Nano-Air fabric is much more air-permeable than windproof synthetic shells. — [Backpacking Light state-of-the-market](https://backpackinglight.com/synthetic-insulated-jacket-reviews-state-of-the-market-report/)
- **Polartec Alpha**:
  - Polartec describes an "open structure… designed to allow exceptional air permeability", with lofted fibres attached to a knit lattice so that "more air-permeable face and back fabrics" can be used. Polartec gives no number. — [Polartec Alpha](https://www.polartec.com/fabrics/insulation/alpha)
  - A Backpacking Light poster cites **60 CFM** from Polartec literature (unverified). The "2 CFM" sometimes quoted alongside it refers to a Pertex shell, not to Alpha. — [Backpacking Light forum](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/88895/)
  - Forum commenters note that tightly woven inner and outer fabrics, chosen for wind or abrasion resistance, reduce whole-garment air permeability. — [Backpacking Light forum](https://backpackinglight.com/forums/topic/polartec-alpha/page/4/)
- **Atom**: Coreloft Compact 60 insulation with an **air-permeable Tyono 20 face**. Breathability is mainly through the stretch-fleece side panels; the Atom Heavyweight's side panels are less air-permeable. — [Casual Geographical](https://casualgeographical.com/arcteryx-atom-vs-patagonia-nano-air-jacket/); [OGL search summary](https://www.outdoorgearlab.com/best-insulated-jacket)
- **Proton**: the updated Proton LT uses **CoreLoft Compact**, and Arc'teryx says it is more breathable than the previous version. The Proton Heavyweight uses a 50D face versus 20D on the standard Proton. — [Mountain Project](https://www.mountainproject.com/forum/message/117778734); [Switchback Proton Heavyweight](https://www.switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-heavyweight-hoody)
- **R1 Air (grid/zigzag fleece)**: hollow-core yarn knit in a zigzag pattern. Channels dump heat and humidity while hollow fibres trap dead air; it is wind-permeable. — [YAMAP マガジン](https://yamap.com/magazine/68296); [山旅旅](https://yamatabitabi.com/archives/154026)
- **Delta**: hollow-fibre fleece that traps heat and breathes. — [AOL/Men's Journal](https://www.aol.com/lifestyle/arcteryxs-warm-lightweight-pullover-hoodie-214500027.html)
- **Synthetic fill and wet**: YAMA HACK says functional synthetic fill keeps warmth while also ensuring breathability (保温性を保ちつつ、通気性も確保). BE-PAL says the Nano Puff's PrimaLoft keeps warmth when damp. — [YAMA HACK](https://yamahack.com/6369); [BE-PAL](https://www.bepal.net/archives/633735)

### Inferences
- The likely physics of clamminess: a tight, low-CFM shell (down or puffy jacket) traps the air layer, so vapour moves only by diffusion. Once sweat production exceeds that rate, humidity rises and condenses near the skin. High-CFM layers let convection carry vapour away. This mechanism is well established in textile science, but I did not retrieve a specific primary source here.
- The Nano Puff, Micro Puff and down jackets in scope fall into the low-CFM category based on reviewer descriptions ("sauna", "no breathability").
- For a user who found the Capilene Cool Daily comfortable, the step-up categories ranked by not-clammy are: **air-knit / grid fleece > Nano-Air-type stretch-woven active insulation > Atom-type hybrid with tighter face > Nano Puff / down**.

### Gaps
- I found no published RET values for any of the in-scope Patagonia or Arc'teryx models.
- There is no official CFM figure for the Proton, Atom, Coreloft Compact, R1 Air, Delta, Kyanite (Polartec Power Stretch) or Polartec Power Air.
- The Outside article "How does a breathable midlayer actually breathe?" was unreachable. — [Outside](https://www.outsideonline.com/outdoor-gear/clothing-apparel/how-does-breathable-midlayer-actually-breathe/?scope=anon)

## 4. Japan Nov–Feb climate framework and layering guidance

### Takeaway
- **Tokyo and Osaka** have mild winters. Daytime highs run about 17–18 °C in November and about 10–12 °C from December to February; lows are about 1–5 °C from December to February.
- **Sapporo** is near or below freezing from December to February (lows about -4 to -6 °C).
- **Nagano** has January highs of 3.8 °C and lows of -3.9 °C.
- **Trains** are heated to about 19–23 °C and get hotter at rush hour, so a breathable layer that is easy to vent or remove beats a sealed puffy for commuting.

**Train heating context**
- Set-points: **Seibu 19 °C, Tobu 23 °C**, Toei Subway 20 °C (the Oedo Line generally runs without heating). Tokyu switches heating **on at 20 °C or below and off above 22 °C**. — [乗りものニュース](https://trafficnews.jp/post/36835)

- The Delta lets "cold air cut through quickly when you stop moving". — [GearJunkie](https://gearjunkie.com/apparel/arcteryx-delta-jacket-review)
- The Proton LT overheats when working hard above about 0 °C (32 °F). — [Switchback](https://switchbacktravel.com/reviews/arcteryx-proton-lt-hoody)
- The Nano-Air Light pairs with a breathable shell in bad weather. — [Better Trail](https://bettertrail.com/outdoor-gear/patagonia-nano-air-light-hoody-synthetic-insulated-jacket-review)
- The Nano Puff is warm enough as the main outer for a mild urban winter. — [tongari-tent](https://tongari-tent.blue/kasendown_matome/)

### Inferences (framework; these are synthesised, not taken from a single source)

**Walking or commuting in Tokyo/Osaka, November (highs 17–18, lows 9–10 °C)**
- Base (Capilene Cool Daily or Capilene Thermal Weight) plus a light air-knit fleece (**Delta / R1 Air Crew**) is enough on most days.
- The fleece alone works as an outer layer.

**Tokyo/Osaka, December–February (highs 10–12, lows 1–5 °C)**
- Daytime walking: base plus **R1 Air / Kyanite Lightweight**. Add a wind shell when it is windy, after dark, or below about 5 °C.
- Standing or waiting outdoors, or cold mornings: base plus **Nano-Air / Proton (Lightweight) / Atom**.
- Trains (19–23 °C, hotter when crowded): unzip or remove the outer layer. A full-zip hoody vents better than a pullover or crew. Avoid a sealed Nano Puff or down layer under a coat.

**Sapporo or Nagano, December–February (highs about -0.4 to 3.8, lows about -4 to -6.4 °C)**
- Active insulation (**Atom / Proton Heavyweight / Nano-Air**) works as a mid-layer under a shell or insulated parka.
- Fleece (**R1 Air / Kyanite**) works as a mid-layer for active use such as hiking or skiing.
- Reviewer evidence that the Proton LT overheats above 0 °C during hard effort supports reserving heavier active insulation for sub-zero conditions or low output.

**Overall heuristic, by highs**
- **10–18 °C**: grid or air fleece.
- **3–10 °C**: active insulation, or fleece plus wind shell.
- **Below 3 °C or windy**: add a shell, or move up to Heavyweight insulation.

These bands are an inference from the normals and the reviewer comments above. They are not a published standard.

- I found no published Japanese temperature-to-layer guide specific to these models. The Outdoor Crunch temperature ratings were not retrievable.
- I found no source on indoor heating set-points for Japanese offices or shops; only train data was found.

## 5. Japanese sizing, where to buy in Japan, and 2026 price news

### Takeaway
- **Patagonia** in Japan sells mainly US sizing, so most Japanese buyers go one size down; the R1 Air in particular is slim-cut. The R1 Air Crew fits closer to Japanese sizing.
- **Arc'teryx** in Japan also typically means one size down from your Japanese size. Exceptions:
  - The Atom Lightweight (ex-SL) is trim, so stay at your usual size or go up if you layer.
  - The Delta fits about the same as Japanese sizing.
  - Arc'teryx's "new fit" size charts are about 3 cm narrower in the chest than the old fit.
- **Sales**: neither brand runs a regular official sale in Japan (Patagonia since 2019). Arc'teryx Japan's big price hikes happened in late 2023 and February 2024, and as of 7 Oct 2026 no new increase had been announced. I found no 2026 Patagonia Japan price-increase report.
